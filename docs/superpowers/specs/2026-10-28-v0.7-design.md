# v0.7: motion, learned — design

## Intent

The project's goal is a virtual cortex: perception that grows out of learned representations with local rules (see the user's direction, recorded in project memory, 2026-10-28: "slower and riskier but the way we want to go"). v0.6 predicts real video well (0.77 × repeating the last frame), but on engineered motion sensing: classic dense optical flow, declared "innate MT". v0.7 replaces it with motion the cortex learns.

The user handed the judgement of what would convince Joscha Bach to Claude ("its up to you"). Claude's answer, accepted by the user ("imo all make sense"):

1. **It grows.** The newborn has no motion cells; direction-selective cells appear with experience.
2. **It is used.** Learned motion replaces optical flow in predicting real video. Silencing it must break the prediction.
3. **One code for self and world.** The same learned motion code carries the eye's own movement, and the efference copy separates it. This part is a stretch goal for v0.7.
4. **Nothing hidden.** No hand-designed motion detector remains in the prediction path.

**Success (Claude's call, told to the user):** v0.7 succeeds if motion is learned and used, even if it predicts real video worse than the optical-flow version. The flow version is kept as the yardstick, and the gap is reported.

## Why v0.3 failed (diagnosis, `reports/cortex/README.md`)

The phase 2 adult's V1 weights on the lagged channel equal its weights on the current one (cosine similarity 0.997–1.000). Sparse coding that reconstructs [current, previous] learns the static pattern twice: separable in space and time, blind to direction. Learning even removed the slight direction preference of random weights: DSI > 0.3 in 19% of untrained cells, 0% after learning.

The literature agrees (research summary in this design's commit message and `docs/research/`). Every working mechanism has one of:
- a target in the future;
- a learning rule asymmetric in time (spike-timing-dependent plasticity as temporal-difference learning, Rao & Sejnowski 2001);
- a multiplicative pairing of frames (motion energy, Adelson & Bergen 1985; gated units, Memisevic & Hinton 2010).

Several lags help too (van Hateren & Ruderman 1998).

## Architecture (Claude's calls)

### 1. V1 unchanged; time lives in MT

V1 is phase 1's adult, unchanged: no lags, no retraining. MT learns over V1's activity at consecutive frames, so time, and the asymmetry it needs, sits in MT. Protocols 1–5 are therefore untouched.

### 2. Motion cells as experts over transitions

- A new level, **MT**, has *M* = 16 motion cell types, convolutional like V1.
- Each type *m* holds a temporal kernel *K_m*: **one non-negative spatial map, shared by all V1 channels**, normalised to sum 1. It is a learned "where does activity go next".
  - It predicts V1's activity at t+1 at every position as the activity at t, moved along *K_m* (a correlation over a (2R+1)² neighbourhood).
  - **Two scales:** V1's own grid (R = 3, ±6 retinal px) and a pooled grid at half resolution (R = 3, ±12 retinal px), each with its own *M* types, so that camera pans are within reach.
- This generalises phase 3's per-command kernels: there the efference copy chose the kernel; here the cortex infers which kernel applies, at every position.
- **Inference:** each position's responsibility *r_m(x)* is a softmax, over cell types, of how well each kernel predicted the transition just seen, smoothed over a small neighbourhood. These responsibilities are the MT activities.
- **Learning (local, asymmetric in time):** each kernel moves by the delta rule toward the transition it is responsible for: (V1 activity at t+1 − its prediction) × V1 activity at t, weighted by *r_m*. This is competitive learning on transitions (EM over a mixture of temporal experts).
  - Its target is the future, the asymmetry the diagnosis calls for.
  - Kernel types start near "no change" with small random differences, so specialisation must come from experience. A newborn has none.
- **What a cell type means is read from its own kernel:** the displacement it predicts is the centre of mass of *K_m*, an interpretable, learned preferred velocity. Because the kernel is non-negative and shared across channels, that centre of mass is well defined.

### 3. Learned flow

- At every position, the population vector Σ_m *r_m(x)* · displacement(*K_m*) gives a velocity, upsampled to the graph's resolution.
- It is used exactly where v0.6 used optical flow: fitted per part (affine), smoothed over three frames, carried forward at half strength.
- **Camera motion is MT's job too.** Phase correlation may still align the graph's canvas, as labelled scaffolding, but it does **not** feed the prediction. Silencing MT must leave the prediction with nothing that knows about motion.
- MT is a new level beside the cortex, used by the video graph. V1's own temporal predictor and phase 3's per-command kernels are untouched.
- The flow version stays as `motion_source="flow"`; v0.7's default is `"learned"`.

### 4. Self and world: moved to v0.8

The eye's command as a prior on which MT type is expected after a saccade needs the eye phase and MT in one network. That is v0.8.

### Non-goals

- Making the cortex itself the next-frame renderer. The scene graph's part-motion path stays; only its motion source changes.
- New V2/V4 levels (objects from learned features): that is v0.8.
- Beating optical flow.

### Anti-goals

Any hand-designed motion detector in the learned path:
- phase correlation;
- Farnebäck;
- Gabor quadrature pairs set by hand;
- direction labels supplied by the experimenter.

Phase correlation still registers the camera in the video graph. That is labelled scaffolding, outside the motion-cell path, and is replaced in a later version.

## Protocols (written before measuring)

- **Protocol 16, motion cells.** Drifting gratings and dots through V1 into MT:
  - (a) the fraction of MT types whose responsibility-weighted response gives DSI > 0.5 ≥ 0.5 in the adult, and ≤ 0.1 in the untrained network;
  - (b) the learned displacements span at least 6 of 8 compass directions;
  - (c) on translating textures, the decoded velocity's direction error, median ≤ 30°.
- **Protocol 17, learned motion used.** Protocol 15's setup, on 40 fresh UCF101 test clips (seed 2, excluding seeds 0 and 1):
  - (a) the learned-motion graph's ratio to repeating the last frame < 1.0;
  - (b) silencing MT (all responsibilities uniform) brings the ratio to ≥ 0.99;
  - (c) the optical-flow version on the same clips is reported, as the yardstick, with no criterion.

Development: protocols 3 and 16 on synthetic drifting stimuli and UCF101 validation; protocol 17's tuning on the validation split only.

## Constraints

- Local learning only, no autograd in the cortex (a test enforces it, as since v0.3).
- Heavy runs on flank (CPU cores, GPUs 0, 1 and 3 only), never on the user's laptop.
- No attribution anywhere. Side branches, merged by PR.
- Failures reported as failures. Engineered parts labelled.

## Builder check: answers (all Claude's calls; the user delegated the decisions)

1. **V1 stays phase 1's adult;** protocol 3 is not re-run. Direction selectivity is tested in MT (protocol 16). v0.7's success rests on protocols 16 and 17.
2. **Camera motion goes through MT,** with two scales for reach. The phase-correlation shift never enters the prediction.
3. **Kernel structure:** one non-negative spatial map per type, shared across channels; displacement = its centre of mass.
4. **MT is a separate level beside V1;** V, V2 and the per-command kernels are untouched. The stretch goal moves to v0.8.
5. **Regressions:** only the video path changes. Protocols 1–14 are untouched; the flow version is re-run inside protocol 17 as the yardstick.

## Left to the builder

Kernel size and neighbourhood (start 7 × 7 offsets at V1 resolution, i.e. ±3 V1 positions = ±6 retinal pixels); the softmax temperature; learning rates; how long to raise the adult (as long as needed on flank); and how MT's two scales are combined into one velocity (Claude's suggestion: per position, the scale whose responsible type predicted the transition better).
