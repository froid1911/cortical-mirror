# v0.10, step 1: grouping learned from common fate — design

## Intent

The object level is the last large engineered piece: slots with pixel templates, found by template search. A virtual cortex should grow its objects from what it has learned. The research brief (`docs/research/2026-10-29-objects-from-learned-features.md`) found no method that does this whole with local rules. Its first recommended step reuses what v0.7–v0.9 learned: grouping links learned by common fate (Prodöhl, Würtz & von der Malsburg 2003).

All calls are Claude's (the user delegated them; the standing goal is to work toward what would serve Joscha Bach).

## Mechanism

- **Features at each V1 position:** the frozen phase 1 V1 code (32 cell types).
- **Common fate from learned motion:**
  - At each place, MT's velocity (the v0.9 MT adult). In the sprite world the eye is still, so there is no self-motion to remove. Later steps add the efference map.
  - Two places share a fate when their velocities are close: s(i, j) = exp(−|v_i − v_j|² / σ²), with σ = 1 retinal pixel per frame. A pair counts when at least one of its places moves (MT speed > 0.2 retinal px per frame); see the amendment below.
- **The learned link, an association field:**
  - A[f, g, Δ] links V1 type f at one place to type g at an offset Δ, within a radius of 4 V1 positions.
  - It learns by a local Hebbian rule: ΔA[f, g, Δ] = η · a_i,f · a_j,g · (s(i, j) − ⟨s⟩), averaged over moving pairs.
  - Pairs that move together strengthen their link; pairs that move apart weaken it. A newborn's A is zero.
- **Affinity on any frame, moving or still:** w(i, j) = Σ_f,g a_i,f · A[f, g, j − i] · a_j,g. What the links learned from motion applies to features alone.
- **Grouping read-out:**
  - Places with V1 activity are joined where w(i, j) exceeds a threshold, and connected components above a minimum size are the groups.
  - The threshold is the 80th percentile of positive affinities in the frame (Claude's call).
  - This read-out is deliberately simple; EM over slots and phase synchrony are later steps.

## Amendment (2026-10-29, before any test measurement)

The first version counted only pairs where *both* places move. A check of MT's speeds on training seed 900 (no test seed was used) showed why that cannot work: in the sprite world nearly all such pairs lie inside one object, share a fate, and give the rule nothing to contrast. The informative pairs are a moving place beside a still one, which do not belong together. The rule now counts a pair when at least one place moves.

The same check set the speed threshold: above 0.2 retinal px per frame are 64% of object places and 4% of background places (at 0.5: 55% and 2.5%). Sprites move 0.25–1.25 retinal px per frame, so 0.5 would miss the slowest.
