# v0.10, step 4: manipulating a scene of learned objects — design

## Intent

Steps 1–3 measured how sharp learned objects are. The development checks after protocol 21 showed that edge sharpness from frame pairs is bounded near the pixel level, whatever the read-out (`reports/cortex/README.md`). Joscha Bach's request was different: a scene graph that explains the world so that it can be manipulated. A scene explains a change when it can say what the world would look like after it: what lies behind a deleted object, and how a moved one looks elsewhere.

Step 4 builds the smallest such scene from the learned parts and measures it with protocol 10's edits. Oversized masks need not hurt here: if the scene remembers the background, deleting a little too much costs nothing.

All calls are Claude's; the user said to keep going while Joscha's answers are awaited (2026-10-29).

## Mechanism (`cortical_mirror/cortex/learned_scene.py`)

- **Objects:** the motion slots (protocol 20), reading MT's fast path (V1's feedforward sweep, gap 4, smoothing 3) and protocol 21's links. Each slot's mask is its region on V1's grid, upsampled to pixels.
- **Background memory:** at every pixel the background slot owns, a running average of what was seen there (rate 0.1), with a count of how often. This is a local rule: each place keeps its own memory. The memory is what makes amodal completion possible: what lies behind an object that has moved before.
- **Edits:**
  - *delete* a slot: its pixels are replaced by the background memory where it has one; where the background was never seen, by inpainting (as the bitmap baseline does);
  - *move* a slot: delete it, then paste its current pixels, under its mask, at the new position.
- **Engineered and labelled:** rendering by pasting, the inpainting fallback, and the slots' EM (as before).
