# v0.10, step 2: objects as slots found by learned motion — design

## Intent

Step 1 (protocol 19) failed: links learned from common fate separate same-object pairs from cross-border pairs (2.3 times, against 1.55 for feature similarity), but in the sprite world a still frame does not show its objects in local features, and a global threshold carves the textured background into patches.

Infants first see objects as what moves together, and only later learn what objects look like (Spelke). The cortex already has learned motion (v0.7–v0.9). Step 2 therefore finds objects where motion is strong, and keeps them where it is not. Once it passes, it replaces the scene graph's engineered template search for finding objects (step 3).

All calls are Claude's; the user delegated them ("ok go ahead", 2026-10-29, after the plan was laid out).

## Mechanism

Everything runs on V1's grid (64 × 48 positions, 4 × 4 world pixels each).

- **Inputs, all learned:**
  - the v0.9 MT adult's velocity, pooled to V1's grid, in retinal px per frame;
  - the frozen phase 1 V1 code;
  - the association field learned in step 1 (`grouping-field.pt`, frozen).
- **Slots.** One background slot and up to 6 object slots. Each slot holds a soft mask over V1's grid (its responsibility q_k) and a velocity u_k.
- **Each frame, a few rounds of EM:**
  - **Prior:** the slot's mask from the last frame, moved by its velocity, then blurred a little. Memory diffuses unless evidence keeps it sharp.
  - **Motion evidence:** exp(−|v(x) − u_k|² / 2σ²).
  - **Link evidence:** exp(β · ā_k(x)), where ā_k(x) is the learned affinity between place x and the places the slot holds, normalised by the frame's spread of affinities. This is how what was learned from common fate holds an object together when motion gives no help.
  - **Responsibilities:** q_k(x) ∝ prior × motion × links, normalised over slots. Each velocity u_k is then the responsibility-weighted mean of v (the background slot's too).
- **Birth:** moving places (MT speed above 0.2 retinal px per frame, as in protocol 19) that the background slot holds most strongly form 4-connected components. A component of at least 6 positions becomes a new slot, if one is free.
- **Death:** a slot whose mass stays below 3 positions for 5 frames is freed.
- **Read-out:** each place is labelled by its most responsible slot; the background slot is label 0.

**Engineered, and labelled as such:** the EM read-out, birth and death are inference machinery written by hand, as the research brief proposed ("EM slot read-out"). What the slots see (V1, MT, the links) is learned. Replacing EM with a learned, local competition is a later step.
