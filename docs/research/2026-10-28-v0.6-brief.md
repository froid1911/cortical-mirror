# Research brief for v0.6: deformable objects and robust curiosity

v0.5 left four protocols failing. Two of them, permanence and grouping recall, trace to tracking that breaks under occlusion. That is engineering, and the fix (probabilistic tracking) is being built separately. The other two are open questions about the approach itself:

1. **Real video:** the graph's expectation is 1.04 × worse than repeating the last frame on UCF101. Predicting camera motion alone reaches 0.99 ×. Most of what remains is non-rigid change (bodies bending, cloth, faces), which rigid sprites cannot predict.
2. **Curiosity:** the learned gaze beats random gaze but misses every criterion. Learning progress measured over one 10-frame fixation is too noisy, and linear features cannot tell an unexplored place from noise.

This brief collects what the literature offers for each, under the project's constraint of **local learning only** (no backpropagation). Citations were checked by web search. Anything that could not be verified is marked.

## 1. Deformable and articulated objects

### Candidates

| Method | What it adds | Local learning? | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Layers with affine motion (Wang & Adelson 1994) | 6 motion parameters per sprite instead of 2: zoom, rotation, shear | yes, least squares + EM | low |
| Smooth flow per layer (Weiss & Adelson 1996; Weiss 1997; Sun, Sudderth & Black 2010) | a smooth deformation field per sprite, fitted by responsibility-weighted EM | yes, same structure as the graph's EM | moderate |
| Flexible / transformed sprites (Jojic & Frey 2001; Frey & Jojic 2003) | appearance in a learned linear subspace, discrete transformations | yes, variational EM | moderate; absorbs appearance change, does not extrapolate motion |
| Articulated parts from motion (Kumar, Torr & Zisserman 2008; Ross, Tarlow & Zemel 2010; Ramanan, Forsyth & Barnard 2006) | an object split into rigid parts joined at their boundaries | yes, EM / greedy structure search | moderate to high; high gain for bodies |
| Active appearance models (Cootes, Edwards & Taylor 2001) | a statistical basis of shapes | PCA, learnable with Oja or Sanger's rule | needs correspondences |
| Gated transformation learning (Memisevic & Hinton 2007) | hidden units that encode the transformation between frames | contrastive divergence (local) | hard at full resolution |
| Form and motion sparse coding (Cadieu & Olshausen 2012) | phase dynamics as motion | local sparse coding | research-grade |
| Slow feature analysis (Wiskott & Sejnowski 2002) | invariance | local | poor fit: it discards the deformation to be predicted |
| Temporal predictive coding (Rao & Ballard 1997; Millidge et al. 2024) | Kalman-like dynamics with local Hebbian learning | yes | low; use over deformation coefficients, not pixels |
| PredNet, First Order Motion Model, Deformable Sprites (Ye et al. 2022) | keypoints + local affine; atlas + per-frame warp | **no** (backprop) | copy the representation, fit it with EM or least squares |

### Caveat

Repeating the last frame is a strong baseline on UCF101. A richer model helps prediction only if the motion it captures *continues* into the next frame. **First measure the headroom:**
- the oracle: fit each deformation model to frame t+1 itself;
- how much of that a constant-velocity extrapolation keeps.

No published number exists for UCF101 with these models.

### Recommended experiments, in order

1. **Affine, then a smooth grid per sprite.**
   - Each sprite gets an affine warp, then a coarse 4 × 4 control grid, solved each frame by responsibility-weighted Lucas–Kanade least squares with a smoothness penalty.
   - Prediction: constant velocity per control point (a Kalman filter per coefficient).
   - Measure the oracle and the extrapolated ratio against the last frame, for rigid vs affine vs grid, on the protocol 15 setup.
2. **Parts by common fate, one level down.** Where a sprite's rigid residual stays high, split its pixels into 2–5 affine sub-layers (Kumar-style), softly attached at shared boundaries. Compare on body-dominated UCF101 classes (JumpingJack, TaiChi, BodyWeightSquats).
3. **A learned deformation basis.**
   - Learn about 8 principal deformation modes online (Sanger's rule) from the grid flows of experiment 1.
   - Predict the next coefficients with a delta rule (temporal predictive coding).
   - Does that beat constant velocity per control point?

## 2. Robust learning-progress curiosity

### What successful systems share

- **Progress is a difference between two windows over many visits to the same region**, never one sample.
  - IAC (Oudeyer, Kaplan & Hafner 2007) warns explicitly that comparing just before and just after one experience is "sensitive to stochastic fluctuations".
  - R-IAC (Baranes & Oudeyer 2009): progress = (sum of errors over the older half of the last ζ samples − sum over the newer half) / ζ.
- **Statistics are per region or per location.** A noise trap is filtered out by its own flat error history: its smoothed derivative is about 0. Global features cannot do this.
- **Selection is stochastic:**
  - proportional to (progress − minimum), plus 20–35% random (IAC ε = 0.35; R-IAC 30% random);
  - SAGG-RIAC (Baranes & Oudeyer 2013) uses |progress|, so that a world that changed draws attention back.
- **Beliefs, not raw error:**
  - Itti & Baldi's Bayesian surprise (KL of posterior from prior, per location);
  - Najemnik & Geisler's ideal searcher;
  - the epistemic value of active inference (Friston et al. 2015).

  All of them habituate to noise, because the posterior absorbs it.
- **The noisy-TV problem:**
  - prediction-error curiosity fails in stochastic settings (Burda et al. 2019);
  - aleatoric discounting, error minus predicted variance (Mavor-Parker et al. 2022), is the most direct fix;
  - ICM (Pathak et al. 2017) ignores what the agent cannot affect;
  - RND (Burda et al. 2019) does not reduce to zero for high-entropy input.
- **Infants** look away from both too-predictable and too-unpredictable sequences (Kidd, Piantadosi & Aslin 2012). Learning progress best predicts when they look away (Poli et al. 2020).

### Recommended experiments, in order

1. **Per-node windowed progress.**
   - Each node keeps a ring buffer of its last ζ per-fixation foveal errors (ζ ≈ 16–24, counted in its own visits).
   - Progress = mean of the older half − mean of the newer half.
   - Gaze is proportional to (progress − minimum), with 25% random, an optimistic start or a 1/√n bonus for new nodes, and |progress| so that change is noticed.
   - First log the noise patch's progress distribution against a learnable object's. If they overlap at ζ = 16, the estimator is still too noisy.
2. **Significance-gated progress.**
   - Reward progress only when z = (m_old − m_new) / √(2 s²/ζ) exceeds 1.
   - Optionally learn a per-node error floor (aleatoric discount).
   - Sweep the noise amplitude: noise capture should stay at or below random at every amplitude.
3. **Expected information gain as a prior, not a learned value.**
   - Each node and place keeps a Gaussian belief with forgetting. A fixation's gain is ½ log(1 + prior variance / noise variance), weighted by foveal visibility.
   - Noise drives the noise variance up and the gain toward 0, while unseen places and newcomers start broad.
   - First as a pure policy against the current learned gaze; then let the three-factor rule learn only the residual. That also prevents learning to avoid everything unknown.

## 3. The question underneath

In v0.5 the learned V1 code found only outlines, so the cortex contributes a principle (normalisation), not learned features, to the scene graph. A cortex that explains the world would grow its objects from learned features, such as surface and texture codes like V2/V4. None of the methods above answers that; the deformation fields and beliefs above would sit on whatever appearance code is used. It is the largest open question for after v0.6.
